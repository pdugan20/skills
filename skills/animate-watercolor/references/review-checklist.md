# Review checklist

Use this after making a bounded watercolor change. Record evidence rather than
checking boxes from memory.

For a new or rejected still, first use the review in
[Still-art direction](still-art-direction.md). Require credible artwork for the
actual decision, inspected against accepted paintings and the intended crop.
Do not run the motion checklist as a substitute for resolving the still.

1. **Accepted reference:** Identify the preserved artwork, preset and checkpoint.
   If matching another subject, read and render its current implementation.
2. **Actual technique:** Name which layers use authored paths, palette deposits,
   source glazes, coverage masks and residual interpolation.
3. **Critical regions:** Inspect before/during/after the reported discontinuity.
   Large coverage and geometry must exist before the final texture settles.
4. **Timing:** Convert the relevant score intervals into visible milliseconds.
   Review normal speed as well as slow motion; do not infer either from the other.
5. **Themes:** Check local whites, clear glass and edge pixels against both real
   site backgrounds. Confirm bloom color and blur behavior.
6. **Composition:** Verify crop, proportions, physical plane and placement at
   desktop and a narrow viewport. Route accessories toward edges hidden by the
   actual entrance/placement. Test actual display-scale bubble visibility,
   not only a zoomed canvas.
7. **Lifecycle:** Replay repeatedly, interrupt, exit/re-enter, change subjects while
   preparing, handle failure/retry, suspend hidden-tab time and honor reduced motion.
8. **Ambient:** Bubbles persist during the hold; the droplet stops at its authored
   endpoint and does not loop. Both restart with a new painting.
9. **Shared system:** Recheck the existing subject when changing common code.
   Keep settings independent and avoid a duplicate player or control panel.
10. **Evidence:** Save source/prompt provenance, chosen parameters, relevant frames
    or recordings, known limitations and the checks actually run. Never use a
    passing unit test as evidence that motion looks natural.

The outcome is a working comparison and a defensible checkpoint. If aesthetic
acceptance is still pending, say so without stopping unrelated authorized work.

## Reject a nominal match

Stage names and a shared renderer are not evidence of matching brush behavior.
When the user cites an accepted sequence, compare at least one initial material
stroke, one shadow return and one detail return in both at the same display scale
and playback rate. Record the visible distinction: touch-down, pressure/width
change, travel, lift-off, pigment depth and source detail. Do not substitute
paused progress samples for watching the temporal sequence.

Reject the new study as a match when its dominant behavior is tracing a tidy
perimeter, filling geometric compartments, wiping finished texture with a broad
brush, or leaving the final settling pass to supply most recognizable detail.
These techniques may be intentional for another style; they do not establish a
match to references built from local paint visits. Inspect coverage separately:
connecting white support can fix holes without improving the pigment gesture.

After a quality rejection, identify the visible failure and its supplying score
or compositor before another revision. A mechanically working candidate may be
reported as a study; do not label it improved or matched solely from tests or
more elaborate code. Check the executable-score diagnostics in
[Material and timing diagnostics](materials-and-timing.md#compare-the-executable-score),
including concurrency, connected ink travel, actual brush flags and effective
parameter changes. Inspect support, pigment and live overlays independently.

Keep tuning controls reachable while reviewing alternative
views, and profile newly introduced background work when existing subjects start
stuttering. Static screenshots or average FPS alone do not prove smooth motion.

Separate cold preparation, warm paint rendering and extra live-overlay work when
attributing stutter. Record the scenario, build, viewport and cache conditions;
do not blame or rewrite the shared renderer from a single slow replay. Preserve
the accepted reference scores and defaults while auditing a new subject.
